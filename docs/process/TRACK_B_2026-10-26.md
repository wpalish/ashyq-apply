### 1.2 The rule: DC-1, a domestic second campus the site's own URLs name

**What was tried first and dropped.** LP-1 ("the listing path confirms only a page typed `PROGRAM_CATALOG`") refuses the
untyped Okanagan page, and touches no returned hit (every hit in the 144 case-columns was typed a programme page). But
a refused listing's own links are followed, and the local replay of ubc on the Exa store then confirmed
`okanagan.calendar.ubc.ca/…/computer-science-bsc`: the same wrong campus, one read later. Refusing a page shape does not
help when the wrongness is the campus.

**DC-1 (`live_discovery.CAMPUS_QUALIFIER_GUARD`, off; harness `--campus-qualifier-guard`, off-form
`--no-campus-qualifier-guard`).** In identity recovery, a lead every other check accepted is refused —
`page names another campus (<place>), not <city>` — when

1. the case's own search results contain two URLs on one host and parent path whose last segments differ in exactly
   one token, one side a word of the institution's city (`entry["city"]`, now carried on `DiscoveryTrace.city`, never
   serialised) and the other not (`computer-science-vancouver-bsc` / `computer-science-okanagan-bsc` → place
   `okanagan`); no word table (`campus_places`); and
2. the page's host labels, URL path, title or `h1` name such a place, or its text names one and never names the city
   (`other_campus_named`).

Without a city, or without such a pair, nothing is learned and the rule cannot fire. CG-1 is unchanged; DC-1 only covers
the campus CG-1 deliberately leaves alone. The confirm stage and the walker are not touched: both ubc pages came through
recovery.

### 2.2 Would a top-up close them

A record request is keyed by text, domain filter and `hl`/`gl` only, so sending exactly these 16 requests once and
merging them into r1 closes every miss above: **16 × $0.022 = $0.35** (Exa wide). But it closes only the paths these
columns took: the replay's path depends on page reads and the clock (kuleuven's pass 2 exists only when a live read
fails), so another run can ask another unrecorded pass. The structural fix is a store that holds both passes for every
case. Serper's base3 does for discovery, which is why the fused store needs no top-up (§3).

## 3. The fused store

### 3.1 How it is built and why interleaved

`python -m evaluation.research.recorded_search --fuse PRIMARY SECONDARY [--order interleave|append] --out FILE` (FUSE-1;
`recorded_search.fuse_documents`). Per request key: a key in one store only is copied; a key in both lists both stores'
URLs, each once (compared without trailing slash, scheme and host case-folded), each result keeping the title and
snippet its own store recorded (Serper's v1 rows have none), ranked by its new position; the primary's `page` record is
dropped once the secondary added a URL; `fused` records how many URLs each gave. Inputs and output pass the store
loader.

**Order: interleave, Exa first at each rank.** Replay hands discovery the top `max_results` = 10 of an entry. Exa's
wide entries hold up to 25 URLs, so "Exa, then Serper's novel URLs" puts Serper's URLs at 11+ wherever Exa returned 10 —
for discovery it is the Exa store plus Serper's 27 Exa-less keys. Interleaving gives each provider about five of the ten
slots, the union's best ranks from both. The append variant was built too (scratch) and compared below.

`recorded_search.fused1.json` = `--fuse recorded_search.r1.json recorded_search.base3.json` (interleave): 290 entries —
147 keys in both, 116 Exa only (topic, scholarship, the sent `local_language` requests), 27 Serper only (among them the
12 dev3 misses), 1 062 Serper URLs added. A test rebuilds it from the two committed stores and compares.

### 3.2 What it answers (offline, retrieval stage)

The production `discover_candidates` on `RecordedSearchProvider`, no fetch, no hop, for every development case with a
known programme: the gold's best rank among the 10 ranked candidates, pass 1 / pass 2 (`m` = the store lacks a request
of that pass, `-` = not among the ten).

| | base3 | r1 | r1t | **fused1** | fused-append |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gold among pass-1's ten | 28 | 31 | 30 | 31 | 32 |
| gold among either pass's ten | 28 | 32 | 30 | 32 | 33 |
| cases with a pass-1 request missing | 1 (ntu) | 1 (imperial) | 0 | **0** | 0 |

Per case the fused list keeps Exa's gains (warsaw 1, ntu 3, kaznu 1, unsw 1) and Serper's (imperial 2, aalto pass 2,
toronto/hku pass 2), and improves Exa's worst ranks (uio 6 → 4, usp 7 → 6, unibo 7 → 4, puc 10 → 7) without reaching
Serper's (uio 1, usp 2, unibo 1). Append keeps r1's ranks and adds only where Exa had fewer than ten.

   decision on the bare host (usp, delft): a per-case twin or the scorer's `www.` row. With them, Exa reads 96 hits / 3
   wrong over the 16 columns against Serper's 96 / 0 (§1.1), and every set is within one case per column of Serper
   (cohort +1, dev2 −0.25, dev3 −0.5, dev4 −0.25).
2. **DC-1 paired on the Exa store** (`rp-dc1-<set>`, §1.2): ubc's wrong → missed, nothing else moves, no new wrong
   claim. Then on by default. That makes Exa 96 / 0.
3. **The miss bar, restated:** discovery misses 0 is not reachable with a single-column record store, because the replay's
   second pass depends on page reads and the clock (§2.1). Accept "≤ the four single-column topic/scholarship misses,
   and every discovery miss named" for r1, or take fused1 as the replay default for **measurement** only (it answers
   every discovery request of either provider) while r1 stays the reference for Exa's own behaviour.

Why r1 over fused1 as the default: fused1 scores no better on programme pages (§3.3–3.4) and it is not what any live
provider returns, so a rule tuned on it is tuned on a list nobody serves; its one gain, closed discovery misses, matters
for the scorecard's row, not for an applicant. Why not Serper's base3: it answers 18 core questions to Exa's 27, its
topic and scholarship searches always miss, and its credits are spent. Tavily: 20 core questions, the worst dev2 (3 hits
a column), 4–6 wrong; not a candidate.

### 4.2 What Exa live costs per applicant run

R1 logged 230 requests for 44 cases (imperial sent none): 150 discovery, 59 topic, 21 scholarship; per case 3 (2 cases),
4 (3), 5 (16), 6 (22); mean **5.23**. Exa's own `costDollars.total` summed over every request: **$5.026**, $0.02185 a
request. Tavily R1′: 232 requests, 5.27 a case, 1 credit each.
