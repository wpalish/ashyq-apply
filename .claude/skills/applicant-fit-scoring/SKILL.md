# Applicant fit scoring

Rank options without ever implying you can predict a decision.

## Keep three judgements separate

Collapsing them hides which one is the problem.

**Eligibility** — do they meet published requirements?
`MET` · `PENDING` · `GAP` · `NOT_APPLICABLE` · `NEEDS_OFFICIAL_CLARIFICATION`

**Admissions fit** — where does the profile sit against those requirements?
`STRONGER_FIT` · `PLAUSIBLE_FIT` · `AMBITIOUS` · `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`

**Funding fit** — what does an official page say is available?
`CONFIRMED_OPPORTUNITY` · `COMPETITIVE_OPPORTUNITY` · `LIMITED_OPPORTUNITY` · `NOT_ELIGIBLE` · `UNKNOWN`

A programme can be eligible and unfunded, or fully funded and out of reach.

## Never produce a probability

No "73% chance of admission". There is no public dataset that supports it, and
a number in that shape will be read as one.

If you produce a numeric score it must be:

- **explainable** — every component visible, with its weight and its reason
- **configurable** — the user sets the weights
- **decomposed** — never a single opaque figure
- **penalised for missing data** — otherwise a sparse profile ties with a verified match
- **labelled** — a preference-match score, stated as not a probability

## Hard filters

Eliminate a programme **only** on a *confirmed* published requirement the
applicant *confirms* they miss:

- the intake is officially not accepting applications
- the published deadline has passed
- a mandatory prerequisite is definitively unmet
- a scholarship is closed to their citizenship
- a published minimum test score is not met

Never eliminate for: a test-optional policy, a missing profile field, a
requirement you could not verify, or a grade on a scale you have not converted
with a documented method.

## Soft scoring

Weight what the applicant said matters: academic fit, funding fit,
affordability against their own stated ceiling, country and city, climate,
workload, programme standing, careers and internships, post-study work rights.

For each component, say what raised or lowered it. "Country preference 1.0 —
Netherlands is on the preferred list" beats a bare number.

## Admissions fit, honestly

Compare the applicant's values to published minimums and describe the margin.

- Clearly above the minimums **and** a substantive activity record → `STRONGER_FIT`
- Above the minimums → `PLAUSIBLE_FIT`
- At or barely above, or a requirement unmet → `AMBITIOUS`
- Nothing verified to compare against → `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`

Every verdict carries the same coda: the decision depends on competitive
selection.

## Activities

Weight by level (school → international), role (participant → founder),
sustained hours (hours per week × weeks per year), and whether there is a
measurable outcome. A two-year leadership role with a stated result outweighs
six memberships.

## Phrasing

Say: *formal requirements are met*; *the profile looks competitive relative to
published data*; *there is a confirmed opportunity to apply*; *the decision
depends on competitive selection*; *there is not enough official data*.

Never say: *you will get in*; *you will receive this scholarship*; *your chance
is N%*; *guaranteed*.
